Yes, with one important architecture correction: your app can absolutely include an AI financial assistant, but a user's existing ChatGPT Plus/Pro subscription does **not** automatically pay for or power AI requests inside your app. ChatGPT subscriptions and OpenAI API usage are separate products with separate billing. ([OpenAI Help Center][1])

OpenAI now also supports **Sign in with ChatGPT** for participating external applications. That can identify/link the user's ChatGPT identity, but by itself it only shares basic identity information such as name, email, and profile picture. It does **not** give your app access to their ChatGPT conversations, memories, files, tokens, or billing information. ([OpenAI Help Center][2])

For your app, I would design **two complementary AI paths**:

1. **AI inside your app.** Your backend calls the OpenAI API. The user's tenant controls what financial data the AI can retrieve. This is how they could ask, “What did I spend on dining last month?”, “Can I afford another $300 investment?”, “Why is my cash flow down?”, or “Build my October budget.”
2. **Your app inside ChatGPT.** Longer term, you could expose your financial app as a ChatGPT-connected app/plugin. Then users could connect their Ledger/Budget account to ChatGPT and ask essentially the same questions from ChatGPT itself. ChatGPT supports connected apps with user authorization and permissions. ([OpenAI Help Center][3])

### One product decision I would make now

I would **not require users to have a ChatGPT account** to use your AI assistant.

Instead:

**Google/email login → your app account → AI available inside Ledger.**

If later they want to link a ChatGPT identity, provide **“Continue with ChatGPT”** as another account-link/login option when the capability is available to your application. OpenAI says Sign in with ChatGPT is currently rolling out across supported external applications rather than being universally available to every third-party developer by default. ([OpenAI Help Center][2])

That gives you a much better launch product because someone shouldn't have to separately purchase ChatGPT merely to get the AI features you're providing.

Your company/application would pay the OpenAI API cost and then decide how to expose it commercially—for example, a limited number of AI questions on the free plan and larger limits on a paid tier. Since API usage is billed separately from ChatGPT, this also gives you control over your unit economics. ([OpenAI Help Center][1])

Longer term, the particularly strong version is **both directions**:

**Ledger → AI:** user opens your budgeting app and asks the financial assistant questions.

**ChatGPT → Ledger:** user connects Ledger to ChatGPT and asks ChatGPT, “How much did I spend last month?” without opening your app.

Building the shared, tenant-secured financial service layer now makes both possible later.
